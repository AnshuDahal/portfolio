01

RESEARCH / RETRIEVAL / EVALUATION

Good answers need good evidence.

MyeloSeq clinical-report chatbot

A question is only as well answered as its evidence.

Helping people understand complex cancer reports means paying attention to the evidence behind every answer.

MY CONTRIBUTION
Evaluation workflow, benchmark design, retrieval comparisons
TOOLS & TECHNOLOGIES
Python · LlamaIndex · RAGAS · RAG

How do we know a helpful-sounding answer is actually supported?

My work on the MyeloSeq assistant at Washington University in St. Louis focused on evaluating retrieval and answer quality. The challenge was to make grounding failures visible, including when a system found relevant text but missed evidence needed to answer the whole question.

What I worked on

  • Built a RAGAS evaluation workflow around the LlamaIndex chatbot.
  • Developed a question benchmark spanning topics and difficulty levels.
  • Compared metadata-enabled retrieval and reranking using answer relevancy, faithfulness, context precision, and context recall.
  • Examined results by topic and difficulty to identify where further retrieval improvements were needed.
A NOTE I’M TAKING FORWARD

An overall score can hide the questions that matter most. Evaluation is most useful when it tells you where to look next.

02

AI FAIRNESS / HEALTH RESEARCH

Look beyond the average.

DDI Dermatology AI Fairness

FST V–VI AUC0.61 → 0.77FALSE-NEGATIVE RATE0.88 → 0.42Reported results · 10-fold evaluation

Better questions about who a model works for.

A model’s overall performance does not tell you whether it works equally well across skin tones.

MY CONTRIBUTION
Training, calibration, evaluation, and reproducible analysis
TOOLS & TECHNOLOGIES
Python · PyTorch · EfficientNet-B3 · Grad-CAM · pandas

Where does a dermatology model perform differently across skin tones?

This project uses DDI and Fitzpatrick17k to investigate dermatology AI performance across skin-tone groups. The work brings training, calibration, validation, and reporting into a reproducible research workflow.

What I worked on

  • Built a workflow for training and evaluating EfficientNet-B3 models.
  • Compared performance across skin-tone groups using a 10-fold evaluation plan.
  • Used Grad-CAM as part of investigating model behavior.
  • Reported changes in AUC and false-negative rates for the FST V–VI group.
A NOTE I’M TAKING FORWARD

Fairness needs to be examined at the group level. The reported FST V–VI AUC improved from 0.61 to 0.77, while the false-negative rate decreased from 0.88 to 0.42 in this evaluation.

03

RESPONSIBLE AI / IN DEVELOPMENT

Support, with room for choice.

Student Wellness Check-In

A work in progress, with people at the center.

An AI-powered wellness application designed around accessibility, safety, user control, and reliable resource matching.

MY CONTRIBUTION
Product workflow and coordination across application and AI workstreams
TOOLS & TECHNOLOGIES
Kotlin · Jetpack Compose · FastAPI · Qwen · Gemini

How can a wellness check-in guide someone toward resources while preserving their control?

The project brings together an Android interface and an AI-backed service for wellness check-ins. The work considers how the experience should behave when a model response is useful, when it is uncertain, and when a fallback is needed.

What I worked on

  • Defined the product workflow and user-facing check-in experience.
  • Coordinated Android, API, and model-routing workstreams.
  • Included structured outputs, evaluation, and fallback behavior in the implementation plan.
  • Kept accessibility, safety, and user choice central to the product design.
A NOTE I’M TAKING FORWARD

The paths around the model matter as much as the model itself: clear choices, reliable resources, and thoughtful fallback behavior.

04

HUMAN-CENTERED AI / FULL STACK

A little space for reflection.

Memory Garden

Technology that makes room for your own voice.

An AI-assisted journaling experience that helps people explore their reflections while preserving authorship and creative control.

MY CONTRIBUTION
AI summarization, persistent context, and accessible interactions
TOOLS & TECHNOLOGIES
React · Node.js · MongoDB · OpenAI API

How can AI help someone reflect without taking over their voice?

Memory Garden explores how a journal can offer assistance while keeping the person writing in charge. It combines a creator-facing interface with persistent context and AI-assisted summarization.

What I worked on

  • Built AI summarization for journal reflections.
  • Used MongoDB to preserve context across the experience.
  • Created accessible interactions for the person writing.
  • Balanced AI assistance with authorship and creative control.
A NOTE I’M TAKING FORWARD

A useful assistant should leave room for the person using it. The design goal is to support reflection while keeping ownership of the story with its author.
